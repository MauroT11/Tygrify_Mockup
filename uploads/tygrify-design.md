# Tygrify - Design & Tone

Source: `Tygify_Website_Requirements.pdf`, returned by the client 4 September 2026.

## Feeling the site should give

The client selected **reassuring and calm** over precise/technical, bold/confident, or friendly/local. Keep this as the tiebreaker whenever a design or copy choice could go either way - the primary visitor has just had a crash and is looking at the site on their phone.

## Colour palette

Sourced from Adobe Color, client's own words: "one strong colour plus a neutral usually works best... save the bright accent colour for your 'Get a quote' buttons."

| Colour | Hex | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accent orange | `#F24405` | Quote and call-to-action buttons only. Not for body text or large fills. |
| Mid grey | `#595959` | Secondary/muted text |
| Near black | `#0D0D0D` | Base background. Client typed `ODODOD` - read as a typo for `0D0D0D`. |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Primary text |
| Light grey | `#B9BAB8` | Cards, dividers, borders |

This is a dark-themed site: near-black background, white text, light grey surfaces sitting on top of it. Check the "reassuring and calm" brief (above) still reads that way on a dark theme once real content is in - a dark UI can tip toward "bold and confident" if contrast and photography aren't handled carefully, and that's the option the client didn't pick. Photography (once received) and generous whitespace/breathing room will matter more here than on a light theme to keep it calm rather than heavy.

Treat this as a starting palette, not a locked one - see "Open design questions" below.

## Reference design received

The client has since shared a reference homepage mockup (received after the discovery pack), which resolves the earlier "no reference sites" gap. Confirmed direction from it:

- Dark theme: near-black background, white text, orange accent - matches the palette already agreed above.
- Bold, high-contrast hero with full-bleed vehicle photography, heavy uppercase display type, and a tagline/subhead pattern ("VEHICLE CARE. WITHOUT COMPROMISE." / "DRIVEN BY A HIGHER STANDARD.").
- Icon-led service strip beneath the hero, before/after slider module, numbered "how it works" section - all reusable patterns regardless of what's underneath them, see `tygrify-site-structure.md`.

**Worth watching:** this look leans toward bold and confident, which is the option the client didn't pick when asked how the site should feel (he chose reassuring and calm). That's not a contradiction to resolve by argument - it's a note to keep in mind through design and photography choices, so the finished site doesn't read as more aggressive than intended once real content replaces the mockup's stock imagery.

## Copy and tone

- Plain English throughout. Define jargon on first use per page - e.g. "SMART repair" gets explained the first time it appears, not assumed.
- Calm and reassuring, not salesy. Avoid urgency-driven or hard-sell language anywhere on the site, including CTAs.
- The founder's story (see business info doc) is a genuine asset - use it fully on the About page. Keep it out of service pages, which should stay practical and specific.
- Use spaced hyphens ( - ) rather than em dashes in all client-facing copy.
- Every factual claim - guarantees, accreditations, turnaround times, prices - must trace back to a confirmed answer from the client. Flag anywhere copy is extrapolating rather than quoting a confirmed fact.
- Target reader for the homepage and service pages: someone standing next to a damaged car on their phone, not a browsing researcher. Write for that reading context - short paragraphs, front-loaded key information (turnaround, price guide, what to do next).
